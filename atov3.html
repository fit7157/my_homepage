Technology

From human movement to meaningful data.

Camera recognition, robot control, and IoT communication work together as one connected system.

HUMAN TRACKING

It sees where the patient is.

MediaPipe detects the coordinates of the patient's body joints. ATOV calculates the difference between the center of the camera frame and the patient's center position. This tracking error is sent to the Arduino, which controls the motors and keeps the robot aligned with the patient.

COMPUTER VISION

Joint coordinates become control signals.

POSTURE ANALYSIS

It notices movement that looks unusual.

ATOV compares the vertical positions of the patient's left and right shoulders. A repeated shoulder imbalance may indicate an abnormal posture. The system can also analyze body movement to identify swaying or instability.

ONE M2M COMMUNICATION

Important information reaches caregivers.

When ATOV detects a fall, abnormal posture, or swaying, the result is sent to a oneM2M-based IoT server. The server stores the event together with its time and location. A smartphone or web application can then display the patient's current condition to doctors and caregivers.

CONNECTED SYSTEM

Three types of data.
One clear view.

The server organizes monitoring information into separate containers for falls, posture abnormalities, and swaying. This structure allows the application to retrieve and display each type of patient data clearly.

Hardware design

Designed.
Printed.
Built.

The main body of ATOV was modeled in SolidWorks and manufactured with a 3D printer. The structure was designed to reduce weight while maintaining enough strength to protect the robot's internal components.

Monitoring process

Follow. Detect. Report.

01

The patient begins walking.

ATOV identifies the patient through its camera and automatically follows while maintaining the required direction.

02

The robot analyzes movement.

Body-joint coordinates are processed continuously to check shoulder imbalance, swaying, and possible falls.

03

The event is saved to the server.

Detected information is uploaded together with the event time and the patient's approximate indoor location.

04

Medical staff check the patient's condition.

Doctors or caregivers can review the information through a smartphone application or monitoring website and respond when needed.

Future development

A continuous connection between patients and care.

Future versions of ATOV can improve autonomous navigation, fall-detection accuracy, indoor localization, and real-time communication with hospital monitoring systems.
